## Abstract

Time-series observations record values but do not ordinarily represent the behaviors occurring within them as explicit computational entities. Boundaries, identities, completeness, and object-level properties must therefore be reconstructed before questions about cycles, episodes, or related accumulations can be answered. We present FeatureGraph, a deterministic framework that transforms ordered observations into sample-level states and events and then into temporally bounded behavioral objects. FeatureGraph v0.1.0a1 implements trough–peak–trough oscillations and wave-derived accumulation in pandas, retaining both object summaries and the construction evidence that supports them. In a clean synthetic verification, the implementation recovered all 12 analytical oscillations with exact boundaries and properties. In a held-out noise study, its tuned directional-state detector maintained mean F1 of 1.00 through noise standard deviation 0.10 but declined to 0.595 at 0.40 as noise fragmented the state sequence. A tuned SciPy peak-based baseline remained more robust, with mean F1 of 0.910 at 0.40. These results distinguish the reliability of detection from the contribution of the resulting representation. Demonstrations using BIDMC respiration and Tennessee Eastman reactor temperature produced the same oscillation schema while retaining domain-specific signals, grouping, preprocessing, and provenance. A reactor-pressure behavioral audit across seven Tennessee Eastman faults and five runs per fault found repeatable early or sustained object-property signatures for Faults 1, 6, 12, and 14, weak signatures for Faults 2, 4, and 7, and no complete Fault 6 post-response objects. These outcomes demonstrate behavioral differentiation and explicit coverage failure rather than superior fault prediction. The alpha shows that, once a behavioral construction is specified, its occurrences can be made explicit, measurable, inspectable, composable, and queryable. It does not provide universal state discovery or a universally superior detector; object reliability remains conditional on the boundary construction used.

## Conclusion

FeatureGraph addresses a representational step that precedes many time-series analyses. Ordered values do not themselves identify behavioral occurrences. By separating observations, sample-level states and events, and bounded objects, the framework makes identity, temporal membership, completeness, properties, and construction evidence available to computation.

The alpha implementation demonstrates this progression for oscillations and wave-derived accumulation. Under construction-aligned clean conditions, it preserved known boundaries and measurements exactly. Under noise, its directional detector became parameter sensitive and increasingly fragmented the signal, while a specialized SciPy peak detector retained stronger object detection. This finding clarifies the framework’s scope: explicit representation cannot compensate for unreliable boundaries, and future implementations should permit detectors suited to the signal rather than treating the alpha construction as universal.

The cross-domain demonstrations establish a different form of generality. Respiration and reactor temperature are not equivalent physical phenomena, but their repeated directional structure can be expressed through the same object schema. This shared representation supports common computational operations while grouping, units, preprocessing, parameters, and scientific interpretation remain domain specific.

The Tennessee Eastman behavioral audit further showed that the object representation can expose heterogeneous process responses without forcing every condition into a predictive label. Several faults produced strong, repeatable changes in object duration, period, amplitude, or transition rate; others produced weak effects; and one produced no complete objects in the later regime. Because all three outcomes remained visible in ordinary tables, the audit supports FeatureGraph's value as an interpretable behavioral record and coverage-aware query layer. It does not establish that the current objects are sufficient for fault diagnosis.

The principal outcome of FeatureGraph v0.1.0a1 is therefore not a transformed signal or a disconnected collection of features. It is an inspectable behavioral record: identified occurrences grounded in observations, supported by explicit states and events, summarized by reproducible properties, related through composition, and available for object-level query. The alpha provides a concrete basis for evaluating whether this representational layer can be extended to broader behavioral types, modular detectors, timestamp-aware measurements, annotated real-world validation, and repositories of behavioral objects across systems.
